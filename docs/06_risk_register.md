# 06 — Risk Register

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Phase |
|----|------|-----------|--------|------------|-------|
| R-01 | **Compression destroys the pulse signal.** Even light compression degrades rPPG heavily; the BBC's independent test found pixelated video made pulse extraction fail. | High | High | Train on **mixed compression** (FF++ raw + c23 + c40) — Intel's own finding is that compression-matched training restores accuracy. Add a quality gate: refuse or low-confidence-flag heavily compressed inputs. **MEASURED 2026-10-20 on FF++ c23: the pulse is not visible — the three face regions disagree by ~8.6 bpm in BOTH real and fake videos, and the colour-ratio methods (CHROM/POS) sit at chance (train-CV AUC 0.52-0.61) while only the raw green channel survives (~0.64-0.70).** **UPDATED 2026-10-20 after a lossless (raw) probe of the same 80 source videos: compression is NOT the cause — at raw quality the spread tightens for both classes (8.6→6.9 real, 8.5→6.6 fake) and the class gap stays zero. Mixed compression is demoted from "the fix" to a robustness check.** | 2–3 |
| R-02 | **Motion & uneven lighting.** The FakeCatcher authors' own false alarms came from movement and lighting, not from clever fakes. | High | High | POS as primary method (illumination-robust); per-frame validity masks; record capture conditions in manifests and report metrics sliced by condition. | 1–3 |
| R-03 | **Clips too short for the math.** 10 s → 6 bpm frequency steps; HR numbers from short clips are coarse. | High | Medium | Hard gate: reject < 10 s (`E_SHORT_VIDEO`). Prefer 30 s windows for HR reporting (2 bpm steps). Always ship a confidence value with the bpm. | 1 |
| R-04 | **Identity leakage flatters scores.** Clips of one person look alike; a random split will lie to you. | High | High | Group-aware splits enforced by a manifest validator that fails the run on leakage (NFR-08). | 2–3 |
| R-05 | **Skin-tone bias.** Plain rPPG performs worse on the darkest skin types; Intel claimed no gap but published no data. | Medium | High | Report subgroup metrics wherever skin-type labels exist; never claim fairness without data; treat diverse test coverage as a release gate, not a footnote. | 3 |
| R-06 | **Pulse-inheriting deepfakes.** The 2025 Frontiers study: high-quality fakes copy the driver's pulse (≈2–3 bpm deviation, face-region correlation ~0.9). Global heart rate alone cannot catch them. | High | High | Design features around **local spatiotemporal consistency** (per-region coherence, PPG-map texture), not the global bpm — this is the paper's explicit recommendation for next-gen detectors. Stress-test with self-made DeepFaceLive fakes. | 2–3 |
| R-07 | **False positives on real video.** The BBC test: every fake caught bar one, but several real videos flagged as fake. A detector that cries wolf is unusable. | Medium | High | Tune the operating threshold on validation and report the full ROC — never a single accuracy. False-positive rate on real videos is a headline metric, not an appendix item. Human review required for any real-world flag. | 2–4 |
| R-08 | **Dataset access delays.** Forms, approvals, Drive rate-limits. | Medium | Medium | Submit all forms in Phase 0; UBFC first (fastest); Kaggle mirrors as fallback. Nothing in Phase 1 blocks on FF++. | 0 |
| R-09 | **Overclaiming results.** "96% accurate" was one older test set; the same paper scored ~91% on harder data. Quoting the best number is how detectors get mis-sold. | Medium | High | Report MAE/AUC **with confidence intervals**, always with the split protocol and dataset named. Cross-dataset numbers are the real score. | 2–4 |
| R-10 | **Scope creep into real-time.** Intel's real-time product ran on Xeon servers with 72 streams; a laptop is not that. | Medium | Medium | Real-time is explicitly out of scope until Phase 4, and even then as batch/offline service. | All |
| R-11 | **Dependency rot.** MediaPipe/NumPy version conflicts, rPPG-Toolbox API drift. | Medium | Low | Pinned `uv.lock`, CI that re-runs the golden-set regression weekly, `numpy<2.0` pin documented in the stack doc. | 0–4 |
| R-12 | **Misuse of the tool.** A working detector can be pointed at real people. | Low | High | Local-only default, no auto-moderation deployment, human-review policy in the charter. This is a research build, not a product. | 4 |
| R-13 | **Decode OOM on real footage.** FF++ originals ship at 1920×1080; a ~900-frame clip is 5.8 GB of `uint8` and the kernel OOM-kills the process (silent SIGKILL, no traceback). Naive restarts look like a mysterious crash loop. | High | Medium | Cap decoded frame height at 480 with the aspect preserved (`ingest.DEFAULT_FRAME_HEIGHT`), enforce `MAX_DECODED_BYTES` so oversized inputs raise `E_UNREADABLE` instead of dying, and keep the decoded buffer to a single preallocated array (no list+stack+flip triple copy). | 1–2 |
| R-14 | **Resolution is a label shortcut in FF++.** Manipulated sequences are re-encoded at a different resolution than their source original (144/200 pairs measured), so a detector can score on resolution instead of blood-flow physics. | High | High | Normalize every video to the same frame height at decode time (480, aspect preserved) and report FF++ numbers as resolution-normalized. Never present a native-resolution FF++ result as a physics result. | 2 |
| R-15 | **The evaluation set cannot resolve the gate.** 56 usable FF++ test videos give a bootstrap CI of roughly ±0.27 on the AUC — the interval spans the 0.70 target, so the experiment can neither confirm nor refute it. | High | Medium | Enlarge the test split (the official FF++ test split holds 140 source videos; only 56 usable so far) before reading anything into a pass or a fail. Report the CI next to every AUC, and state "too small to resolve" when the interval spans the gate. **RESOLVED 2026-10-20: test split enlarged to 250 usable videos (all 140 official test originals + 115 manipulated, methods balanced). The pooled-AUC CI tightened to ±0.065 and now excludes 0.70 — the gate is measurable and it fails (0.5434).** | 2 |
| R-16 | **Feature-window size silently reweights the population.** A 10 s analysis window drops clips shorter than 10 s, and the FF++ fakes are re-encoded (so they are shorter more often): a 15 s window kept 50% of the test fakes but 69% of the test reals. Selecting on the resulting AUC rewards a biased subset. | Medium | High | Coverage guard in the detector search: a candidate window size must retain ≥95% of the videos of every class in every split, and label-biased shrinkage is reported in the run report. | 2 |
| R-17 | **One manipulation family can carry the whole result.** On the enlarged test split the pooled AUC (0.543) is an average over one detectable family (Deepfakes 0.730) and three blind spots (FaceSwap 0.595, Face2Face 0.459, NeuralTextures 0.394 — the last *anti*-correlated). Quoting the pooled number alone hides that; a detector can also be confidently wrong on a whole family. | High | High | Always report per-method AUC with bootstrap CIs next to the pooled number, and state the mechanism gap (pulse inheritance, R-06) rather than presenting a single averaged score. | 2 |

**Top 3 to internalize before writing code:** R-04 (leakage — silently invalidates
everything), R-06 (pulse inheritance — defines what features are worth building),
R-01 (compression — defines what data you train on).
